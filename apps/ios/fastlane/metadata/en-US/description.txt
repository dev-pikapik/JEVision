JEVision Remote turns your iPhone into a remote for JEVision, a free and open-source Mac app that tests the interface of any Mac app the way a person uses it: it looks at the screen and works the real mouse and keyboard. Each test is a flowchart of simple blocks.

Pair the iPhone with JEVision on your Mac once, by scanning a code, and then:

• Browse the projects and tests on each paired Mac.
• See a test drawn as its flowchart, with its card: description, type, priority, status and author.
• Read the sticky notes on a test, with their replies and links.
• Start a test, or one row of its data table, follow it block by block, or stop it.
• Edit block settings, add or delete blocks, and rename tests when the Mac allows it.
• Read reports with a short reason for each failure, and open screenshots only when you need them.
• Look through a project's run history, and see runs that are waiting for the Mac's screen.

NO MAC AT HAND?
Tap See an Example on the first screen to look around a Mac built into the app: tests, notes, a run to start and a failed check to read. The example works without a network.

WHAT YOU NEED
The tests run on a Mac with JEVision, which needs macOS 26 or later. JEVision for Mac is free and open source, and you download it from GitHub: https://github.com/dev-pikapik/JEVision/releases

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• No account and no sign-in. No ads, no tracking, no analytics.
• Tests, pictures and secrets stay on the Mac. The iPhone fetches only what you open and keeps nothing but the list of paired Macs.
• The connection is end-to-end encrypted, directly on the same Wi-Fi or through a relay that can't read it.
• The iPhone proves who it is with a key in its Secure Enclave that never leaves it.
• The app locks with Face ID or your passcode, and the Mac decides whether an iPhone may view, run or edit tests.

JEVision and JEVision Remote are open source under the MIT license.
